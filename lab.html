Balance Lab

Runs the real game rules with no map drawing. Pick a test, press Run, read the tables. Every run is seeded, so the same settings give the same result. Targets live in lab/balance-targets.js; a result outside its range is red. Red results are reported, not hidden by retuning numbers.

Unit duels

Side A against side B on a small test ground, 110 m apart, either side optionally as one squadron (cohesion, shared targets, halting on contact), both attack-moving towards each other, until one side is gone or 3 minutes pass. "Height" puts side B 30 m higher at the crest of a ramp; "forest" puts side B inside a forest strip.

Fortifications

Trench hold: 5 Riflemen hold a 120 m trench (or the same spot in the open, for comparison) against attack-moving Riflemen; with grenades, the attackers are sent in again and again to throw at the nearest defender. Bunker assault: a full Bunker, 5 men inside (4 Riflemen and a Machine Gunner), against Riflemen who are sent in again and again to throw grenades at it (1 sulfur each, 25 m reach, 1 s wind-up, 20 s cooldown). The attackers win when the Bunker falls or everyone inside is dead. There are no agreed targets for these yet.

Economy timeline

A standard opening on Highland Pass with the enemy and neutrals removed: a Lumber Camp at the nearest forest, a Mine on the nearest iron, two Workers each, then Workers from the HQ until there are eight. Nothing else is bought. "Tier II affordable" uses the Heavy Machine Gun's cost, the cheapest Tier II item. Harvest runs at the game's pace (x0.7).

Meta snapshot

A fixed set of seeded tests that sums up the balance of this build: equal-supply duels between every armed unit (six of each soldier, three Mortar Crews), terrain and squad duels, the fortification tests, the economy timeline, and how hard the enemy commander pushes a player who stays home (30 game minutes per difficulty). Each result is shown next to the saved baseline from patch 0.5b (lab/meta-baseline.js), so a later patch can be compared number by number. A full run takes several minutes.

AI versus AI

The scripted commander plays both sides of Highland Pass. Side 1 gets the same start as the enemy (resources, Barracks, Ordnance Works, garrison). The map is not symmetric, so this also shows whether the base or the mountain is favoured. These results only describe the scripted AI until it follows the player's economic rules. Early minutes run at about five game minutes per real second; once both armies march it slows to about five game minutes per real minute, so a full match takes several minutes. Fog of war is the cost; patch 0.7 makes it incremental. Two identical scripted AIs currently stall with no winner, so the match-length target waits until the AI plays by the player's economy.
